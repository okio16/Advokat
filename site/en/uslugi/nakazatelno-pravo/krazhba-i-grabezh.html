The difference between theft and robbery

Theft is taking someone else's movable property without the owner's consent with the intention of appropriating it. Robbery is taking property by using force or threats. It is the force or threat that makes the act a much more serious crime, which is why the main dispute in the case is often whether there was any violence at all.

Minor cases and compensation of damage

Theft of low-value items without aggravating circumstances may be classified as a minor case and punished much more leniently. The law also encourages compensation – if the stolen property is returned or paid for by a certain stage of the proceedings, the sentence is reduced significantly.

Compensation is also important for subsequent rehabilitation and in many cases opens the way to a plea agreement with the prosecution.

How I build the defence

  1. Checking video footage, witnesses and identification records.
  2. Assessing whether the value of the items was determined correctly – expert valuations are often inflated.
  3. Assessing whether it is a minor case or a lighter legal qualification applies.
  4. Negotiating a plea agreement when the evidence is strong, or contesting the charge fully when it is not.

Frequently asked questions

If I return the stolen items, will the case be closed?

Not automatically, but compensation is a strong mitigating factor and in some cases leads to a much lighter sentence or a plea agreement.

What is a minor case?

A case where, due to the low value, the absence of harmful consequences and the personality of the offender, the danger to society is clearly lower. The penalty is then much lighter.

Can theft be reclassified as robbery and vice versa?

Yes. If it is proven that force was used only to escape and not to take the property, the qualification may change – one of the main disputes in these cases.
