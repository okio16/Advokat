When drink-driving is a crime

Bulgarian law distinguishes between an administrative offence and a crime. Driving with a blood alcohol level of over 0.5 and up to 1.2 per mille is an administrative offence under the Road Traffic Act – with a fine, penalty points and disqualification from driving. Over 1.2 per mille it is a crime under the Criminal Code. Driving after the use of narcotic substances is also a crime.

0.5 to 1.2 ‰Administrative – the penalty decree is appealed
Over 1.2 ‰Criminal – pre-trial proceedings and court

Refusal to be testedSevere administrative consequences, and under certain conditions criminal ones

Apart from the penalty, two further questions arise – disqualification from driving and the possible confiscation of the car by the state in certain situations provided by law. This is where the defence matters most.

The evidence I check

  • Whether the breathalyser was approved, calibrated and used correctly
  • Whether you received a referral for a medical test and how soon the blood was taken
  • Whether there is a discrepancy between the breathalyser and the blood test
  • How the concentration at the time of driving was calculated
  • Who owns the car – relevant to possible confiscation

A difference of a few tenths of a per mille may mean the difference between an administrative offence and a crime. That is why a blood test is essential when the breathalyser shows a value close to the threshold.

Possible outcomes

With a clean record and no injured persons it is often possible to end the case with a plea agreement or summary trial and a lighter penalty. When there are procedural violations or doubts about the evidence, the case is fought to the end. Where there was an accident, compensation issues arise as well – see road accident compensation.

Frequently asked questions

Should I give blood if the breathalyser shows over 1.2‰?

It is usually advisable, especially if the value is close to the threshold. The blood test is more accurate and may refute the breathalyser.

Will they take my car?

The law provides for confiscation of the car in certain cases. Whether it applies depends on the specific circumstances and the ownership of the car – a question where the defence matters.

How long will I be without a licence?

The period is set by the court within the limits of the law and depends on the concentration, previous record and circumstances. For an administrative offence the disqualification is under the Road Traffic Act.
