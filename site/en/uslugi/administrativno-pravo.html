When the administration gets it wrong

Every day state and municipal authorities impose fines, issue orders, refuse permits and carry out inspections. Most of these acts can be appealed. The court checks whether the act was issued by a competent authority, under the correct procedure and on sufficient evidence.

For six years I worked as a legal adviser at the Commission for Consumer Protection and represented the Commission before the courts. I know “from the inside” how acts are drafted, what mistakes are made and what the court looks for when reviewing them. Today I use this experience to protect individuals and businesses.

Administrative penalty cases – fines and pecuniary sanctions

Administrative penalty proceedings begin with a report establishing an administrative violation (AUAN) and end with a penalty decree, an electronic fine or an on-the-spot fine. The deadlines are short and cannot be restored.

DocumentDeadlineWhere
Violation report (AUAN) – written objections7 days from drawing upTo the authority that drew up the report
Penalty decree – appeal14 days from serviceDistrict court, via the authority that issued the decree
District court judgment – cassation appeal14 days from notificationAdministrative court

The most commonly appealed are fines under the Road Traffic Act and sanctions from the National Revenue Agency, the Consumer Protection Commission, the Road Transport Administration, the Labour Inspectorate, municipal ordinances and environmental inspectorates. The procedure is described in detail on the appeals against penalty decrees page.

If you win the case against a penalty decree, the court awards you your lawyer's fees. This makes an appeal reasonable even for smaller fines.

Administrative acts and orders

Individual administrative acts directly affect your rights – refusal of a permit, an order to demolish a building, a coercive administrative measure, withdrawal of a licence, dismissal of a civil servant. They are challenged under the Administrative Procedure Code before the administrative court, usually within 14 days of notification.

  • Orders imposing coercive administrative measures (taking a vehicle off the road, temporary withdrawal of a driving licence, sealing of premises)
  • Orders under the Spatial Development Act – demolition of buildings, refusals to legalise
  • Refusals to issue licences, permits and registrations
  • Orders dismissing civil servants – see unlawful dismissal
  • Tacit refusals – when the administration does not reply at all

More about the procedure on the appeals against administrative acts and orders page.

Tax disputes and audits

A National Revenue Agency audit is stressful for any business. The tax audit act is first appealed administratively before the Director of the “Appeals and Tax and Social Security Practice” Directorate and then before the administrative court. I assist already during the audit – with submitting documents, objections to the audit report and preparing the appeal. See tax disputes and audits.

Compensation from the state under the State Liability Act

The State and Municipalities Liability for Damage Act gives the right to compensation when you have suffered from an unlawful act or action of the administration, an unlawful charge or detention, or excessively slow justice. More on the State Liability Act cases page.

Frequently asked questions

Should I sign the violation report (AUAN)?

You may sign it with objections or refuse. Signing is not an admission of guilt. What matters is to record your disagreement and file written objections within 7 days.

Can I appeal an electronic fine?

Yes. An electronic fine for a violation recorded by a camera is also subject to judicial review within 14 days of service.

How much does it cost to appeal a fine?

The fee depends on the complexity and is set in line with Ordinance No. 1. If you win, the court orders the penalising authority to reimburse your lawyer's fees.

Does the appeal suspend payment of the fine?

Yes – until the penalty decree becomes final, the fine cannot be collected by force. Coercive administrative measures, however, are often enforced immediately.

What is a “minor case” for an administrative violation?

When the violation poses insignificant danger to society, the authority or the court may decide not to impose a penalty and only warn the offender.
